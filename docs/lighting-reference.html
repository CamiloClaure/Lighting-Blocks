Collection loop

Every fundamental particle is warm yellow (#FFD66B) and picked up by walking over it, within a 1.1 unit radius. A pickup is consumed once and adds one particle to a single shared inventory (not per island), capacity 30. Pause, losing window focus, and any blocked-gameplay state (menus, dialogs) all prevent collection. Collecting never happens if the shared inventory is already full - the world pickup stays put.

Space is only for carrying and placing bridge blocks. There is no particle dragging and no particle/block grab arbitration.

Pressing E deposits one particle into the nearest receiver within 1.5 units that has an unobstructed line to the player. One press affects one receiver; holding E does not repeat the deposit.

Receivers and rewards

Each Wisdom Tree has three color receivers, one per color, each charging independently from 0/3 to 3/3:

ColorTier
Blue0
Violet1
Amber2

Depositing two blue particles and one amber particle leaves that tree's receivers at blue 2/3 and amber 1/3 - the receivers do not share a bank. At 3/3, a receiver queues one tile of its own color at its tree. Multiple receivers that complete at the same time are processed in the order they completed, through the tree's existing charge animation and output slots.

A full receiver (3/3, awaiting its tile) rejects further deposits until the tile actually appears. If the tree has nowhere to place a tile (output slots blocked), the charge and the queue entry are both kept exactly as they are - a receiver's charge is only spent once its tile is successfully created, so nothing is lost while blocked.

Returning a loose, unplaced tile (R near its Wisdom Tree) refunds three yellow particles into the shared inventory, subject to the same 30 cap: the tile is not destroyed if its full refund cannot fit.

Islands and floor

Island tiles are opaque, depth-writing URP Lit - no placeholder outlines, filaments, or emission. Only the player's and the trees' own lights illuminate the surface.

Receivers place deterministically per island by farthest-point selection over reachable supported ground, kept clear of edges, bridge landings, tree outputs and main walking routes. The tunables live as consts in code, not Inspector fields, in Assets/LightingBlocks/Runtime/LightingParticles.cs:

ConstValueMeaning
ReceiverSpacing8fMinimum distance kept between two receivers on the same island.
ReceiverClearance2.2fRadius around each receiver kept clear of particle placement/respawning.
DepositRange1.5fHow close (unobstructed) a receiver must be for E to reach it.

Shared inventory capacity is also a code constant, not an Inspector field: ForestWorld.InventoryCapacity = 30 in Assets/LightingBlocks/Runtime/ForestWorld.cs.

Visibility

Visibility is limited by horizontal (XZ) distance from the player, not the camera, so zooming or orbiting out never reveals anything farther away. Set on the LightingFog component, on the GameManager object:

FieldDefaultMeaning
World Clear Distance6World reads at full brightness within this many meters.
World Black Distance12World is completely black at this many meters.
Particle Clear Distance4Particles read at full color within this many meters.
Particle Hidden Distance6Particle renderers switch off beyond this many meters (about three floor tiles).
Particle TintblackColor particles fade toward as they leave clear range.

The opaque-world fade is a render pass, not a component setting: FullScreenPassRendererFeature named "Lighting Fog" on Assets/LightingBlocks/Art/Lighting/LightingRenderer.asset, using material M_LightingFog.mat and shader Assets/LightingBlocks/Shaders/LightingFog.shader. It runs after opaques/skybox and before transparents and post-processing. Transparent crystal, filament, edge and effect shaders fade the same way, gated by the global shader property _LightingFogActive (0 outside Lighting, so legacy scenes that share those shaders are unaffected).
